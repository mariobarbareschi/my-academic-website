Research perspectives

Research

Engineering trust, resilience and intelligence into the computing systems that shape the physical world.

As computing moves into sensors, vehicles, industrial infrastructure and everyday devices, security, reliability and efficiency become inseparable design challenges. Trust begins at the hardware level; resilience must extend across the whole system; intelligence increasingly needs to operate where data is produced.

Research at this intersection connects physical devices, digital circuits, processor architectures and embedded software. The emphasis is on measurable properties and informed trade-offs: how systems establish trust, how failures affect their behavior, and how much can be achieved with limited energy, memory and computational resources.

Focus areas

Research directions

Four connected perspectives on a central question: how can computing remain trustworthy and effective when hardware, resources and operating conditions impose real limits?

01 / Security

Hardware Security & Trust

How can a connected device prove its identity without relying entirely on secrets stored in memory? Physical Unclonable Functions (PUFs) exploit tiny, device-specific variations in electronic hardware to support authentication and cryptographic key generation. Their characterization and integration into secure protocols help establish hardware-rooted trust in IoT, embedded and distributed systems.

PUF characterizationHardware roots of trustSecure protocols
02 / Dependability

Reliability, Testability & Resilience

Even correctly designed software can produce incorrect results when the underlying hardware is affected by defects, transient faults or aging. Reliability, testability and resilience research examines how failures originate, propagate and influence processors, specialized accelerators and entire applications. This understanding supports more dependable embedded intelligence and safety-critical computing.

Reliability assessmentFault detectionTesting methods
03 / Architectures

Efficient Edge AI & Embedded Architectures

Artificial intelligence is moving from remote data centers to devices that sense and act in real time. Yet edge platforms have strict limits on memory, energy and processing power. Architecture-aware inference studies how algorithms interact with processor features, vector instructions, accelerators and memory hierarchies to make on-device intelligence faster, more efficient and dependable.

On-device inferenceResource-aware executionArchitecture-level optimization
04 / Design methods

Reconfigurable & Approximate Computing

Computing hardware does not always need a fixed architecture—or more numerical precision than an application can use. Reconfigurable computing adapts hardware to specific tasks, while approximate computing explores controlled trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency. FPGA design, circuit synthesis and design-space exploration make it possible to compare solutions for speed, resource use and energy, including emerging in-memory technologies.

FPGA designApproximate circuitsDesign-space exploration
Research in practice

From methods to real systems

The significance of a computing method is ultimately tested in real systems. These areas converge where security, timeliness and efficiency are not separate features, but requirements that must work together.

  • Embedded, IoT & Edge-to-Cloud Systems

    From intelligent sensors to distributed infrastructures, connected systems must process information close to its source while preserving security, efficiency and continuity across the edge-to-cloud continuum.

  • Railway & Safety-Critical Applications

    Railway and industrial infrastructure place particularly demanding requirements on dependable computation. Research and development activities connect custom FPGA-based hardware, real-time embedded software and systematic safety-oriented evaluation, including the scientific leadership of selected industrial projects.

  • Prototypes & Research Tools

    Experimental FPGA platforms and design-automation tools turn architectural ideas into testable systems. Research tools including IIDEAA and XbarGen support the exploration, implementation and evaluation of alternative hardware designs.

Methods & evidence

Research contributions

Beyond the research questions lie the methods, evidence and tools used to address them. These complementary contributions connect hardware phenomena with measurable system behavior, from device authentication to reliable and efficient computation.

Hardware-rooted trust

Silicon-level variability becomes a useful security primitive when it can be characterized and incorporated into robust system designs. PUF evaluation is connected with authentication, key management and trust establishment for devices with limited hardware resources.

Hardware characterization · Authentication · Key management

Dependability across abstraction levels

Assessing dependability requires tracing the effects of failures across abstraction levels. Reliability and testability methods connect component-level faults, architectural behavior and application-level outcomes, including those of specialized hardware accelerators.

Fault effects · Testability · Reliability assessment

Architecture-aware embedded intelligence

Efficient embedded AI is more than a matter of shrinking a model. Execution kernels, processor capabilities and data movement determine whether an inference workload can meet real-time constraints. Architecture-level analysis makes trade-offs in latency, memory footprint and computation explicit.

On-device inference · Embedded architectures · Efficiency

Design-space exploration for efficient hardware

Efficient hardware design is a multi-objective problem. Automated exploration, circuit transformations and controlled approximation make implementation alternatives comparable across complexity, performance, energy use and computational accuracy, from FPGAs to emerging devices.

Reconfigurable design · Approximation · Emerging technologies
